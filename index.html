    Learning Research Initiative

    Beyond education. Towards understanding.

    We're studying how people learn, where learners struggle and why progress changes, using assessment records, topic-level analysis and published research. Marks show an outcome; we want to understand what happened behind them.

    1

    Observe

    Record assessment outcomes, question-level responses and the conditions under which each test was taken.

    2

    Analyse

    Map responses to topics through question-paper analysis and document-based topic percentage mapping.

    3

    Investigate

    Ask better questions about understanding, recall, reasoning and independent application, without assuming the cause.

    Assessment framework: 3 levels, 195 questions, 900 marks

    Read this honestly. These are the recorded results of a single learner in a personal pilot (449 / 900 overall, 49.89%). They are not a representative sample and prove nothing about students in general. The 60% reference baseline is a proposed project reference point, not a validated standard. Several topic percentages rest on very few questions, so we publish the counts next to every percentage.

    What the global industry says

    We review company surveys, programme reports and independent academic experiments, and label each type of evidence so company-reported data isn't mistaken for scientific proof.

    ≈

    Company-reported

    Google, Microsoft, IBM, Amazon India and Apple: surveys, research collections and programme reach. These describe usage and attitudes, not proven learning gains.

    ⚗

    Randomized studies

    OpenAI's student study and peer-reviewed work in PLOS ONE and PNAS test specific interventions, and show that assisted performance isn't the same as independent learning.

    !

    Stated limits

    Every source is presented as external reference, not DGBP research, and our pilot figures are never compared directly with these studies.
