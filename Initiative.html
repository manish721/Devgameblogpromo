01 · Research Initiative

Learning should be understood, not just measured.

Marks can show an outcome, but they do not always explain what happened during learning. Our initiative looks beyond a final score to examine topic-level strengths, repeated errors, unanswered questions and changes in performance.

01

Observe

Record assessment outcomes, question-level responses and the conditions under which the assessment was completed.

02

Analyse

Map responses to topics, identify patterns and compare performance across assessment stages.

03

Investigate

Develop research questions about understanding, memory, study practices, reasoning and independent application.

02 · Purpose

Why is DGBP here?

DGBP is here to investigate learning challenges and explore ways to make learning more effective. We are not here merely to test students or label them by marks.

Our central question

When a learner struggles, what exactly is happening? Is the difficulty related to a topic, a missing prerequisite, the way a question is understood, the learning method, recall, application or something else? We want to investigate these possibilities instead of assuming a cause.

Identify where learners struggle

Use question and topic-level records to locate areas that may need further attention.

Understand how people learn

Explore learning, practice, feedback, recall, comprehension and application.

Understand stalled progress

Investigate why a learner's performance may change between assessments.

Examine education challenges

Study learning barriers and differences between classroom exposure and demonstrated understanding.

Explore study techniques

Review methods and ask whether they help learners understand and retain material.

Support better outcomes

Use evidence to guide improvements rather than relying on assumptions or promises.

03 · Direction

Mission & Vision

MISSION

Investigate learning with evidence

To observe learning challenges, analyse assessment responses, explore learning methods and share clear, honest findings that can inform better educational practice.

VISION

A more thoughtful learning ecosystem

To contribute to an education environment where learners are understood as individuals, learning is evaluated across multiple dimensions and decisions are informed by evidence.

04 · Research Questions

Questions that guide our investigation

These are questions to investigate, not conclusions already established by DGBP.

Understanding

Can learners explain a concept in their own words and use it in a new situation?

Memory & recall

What can learners recall after a period of time, and how does practice relate to recall?

Reasoning

Can learners identify the steps needed to solve unfamiliar problems?

Study approaches

How do different study approaches relate to accuracy, comprehension and retention?

Progress over time

What patterns appear when the same learner completes multiple assessments?

Independent learning

Can learners demonstrate what they know without hints, tools or external assistance?

05 · Learning Dimensions

Learning is multidimensional

A score is one signal. A broader picture may require several different observations.

Knowledge

Recognising and recalling concepts, definitions, facts and procedures.

Comprehension

Explaining meaning, relationships and underlying ideas.

Application

Using learned ideas to solve problems or complete practical tasks.

Reasoning

Connecting evidence, selecting methods and explaining decisions.

Retention

Recalling and applying knowledge after a delay, not only immediately after study.

Independent performance

Demonstrating learning without outside help or answer-generating tools.

06 · Research Process

From observation to informed questions

1

Define the learning area

Identify the subject, topics, learning objectives and assessment scope.

2

Collect responses

Record marks, correct answers, incorrect answers and unanswered questions.

3

Map topics

Organise questions by topic and calculate topic-level performance where records permit.

4

Compare stages

Review changes between tests while considering differences in question count and difficulty.

5

Investigate patterns

Check question wording, recurring errors and possible prerequisite gaps before interpreting results.

6

Report limitations

State what the data can show, what it cannot show and what further evidence is needed.

07 · Proposed Learning Profiles

Patterns to explore, not labels

These are descriptive possibilities for analysing evidence. They are not diagnoses, fixed student categories or validated psychological profiles.

Consistent performance

Similar performance across topics or repeated assessments.

Topic-specific difficulty

Stronger results in some topics and weaker results in others.

Recall difficulty

Possible difference between recent learning and delayed recall, requiring a suitable retention assessment.

Application gap

Correct recognition of concepts but difficulty applying them to unfamiliar questions.

Variable performance

Large changes across rounds or assessments that need further investigation.

Developing performance

Evidence of improvement across comparable tasks, interpreted with care.

Proposed reference baseline

60%

For the current framework, 60% is used as a proposed project reference point: 180/300 per level or 540/900 overall. It is not a universal or externally validated standard.

Exemption test

20 questions

The exemption test is separate from the three-level, 900-mark total. Marks per question and the qualifying threshold have not been confirmed, so no total score is assumed.

Assessment conditions to document

Four-option MCQs with one correct answer; no partial marks; no negative marking; unanswered questions receive zero; no external assistance or hints; answer key hidden until submission; explanations shared after evaluation; and different but equivalent question sets where applicable. Duration, breaks and permitted materials must be specified before a formal comparison. The current plan proposes completing all three main assessments on one day.

09 · Analysis Strategies

Two strategies we follow

DGBP uses question-paper analysis and document-based topic percentage mapping to examine performance at more than one level.

STRATEGY 01

Question paper analysis

Inspect each question and the learner's response to understand where errors or omissions occur.

  • Identify correct, incorrect and unanswered responses.
  • Review question types and wording.
  • Look for repeated errors or misconceptions.
  • Check whether the error appears topic-specific or question-specific.
  • Use the answer key and explanations after submission.
STRATEGY 02

Document-based topic percentage mapping

Maintain a structured record linking each question to a topic and calculating performance where the underlying counts are available.

  • Record topic name and number of questions.
  • Record correct answers and total attempted/available questions.
  • Calculate topic percentage transparently.
  • Compare topic patterns between tests when comparable data exists.
  • Use the document to identify areas for further review.

Percentage calculation

Topic accuracy = (correct answers ÷ total recorded questions for that topic) × 100. Always display the number of questions alongside the percentage, because a result based on one or two questions is much less stable than a result based on a larger sample.

11 · Global Industry Research & Verified Evidence

What does the global industry say?

DGBP reviews published research, company surveys, educational programmes and independent academic experiments. Each type of evidence is labelled so that company-reported data is not confused with independent scientific proof.

How to read this section

A company survey can describe reported usage or attitudes. A programme announcement can document reach. A randomized experiment can test a defined intervention under specific conditions. These are different forms of evidence and should not be treated as interchangeable.

Google · Learning science

Google for Education — Research and Learning Science

Research collection · Methods vary by study

Google's research portal describes its use of observational studies, randomized controlled trials, user feedback and expert evaluations. Its learning-science principles include active learning, retrieval practice, dialogic inquiry, problem-solving, managing cognitive load, curiosity and motivation.

DGBP relevance: These concepts provide useful questions for examining active recall, learner explanations, cognitive load and engagement. The portal is a collection, not one single experiment or one universal effect size.

Microsoft · 2026 AI in Education Report

AI adoption across education

Company-reported international survey
3,345 respondents

Microsoft says PSB Insights surveyed 3,345 respondents across K–12 and higher education in the United States, United Kingdom, Australia, Brazil, Japan and Saudi Arabia. The online survey ran from February 13 to March 9, 2026.

Microsoft reports that 92% of students and education leaders and 88% of educators had used AI for school-related purposes. It also reports that 77% of students and 53% of educators had not received formal AI training.

DGBP relevance: Adoption and training are worth studying separately. The figures are survey responses reported by Microsoft; they do not prove that AI use improves learning outcomes.

OpenAI · Student learning study

Understanding AI and Learning Outcomes

Randomized study · More than 300 college students
300+

OpenAI describes a randomized study of more than 300 college students preparing for neuroscience and microeconomics exams. Participants were assigned to traditional online resources or to one of two Study Mode variants.

OpenAI reports that microeconomics students assigned Study Mode access scored roughly 15% higher relative to the no-AI control group. Neuroscience results were directionally positive but not distinguishable from the control group.

DGBP relevance: Results differed by subject. The study also highlights the need to measure whether short-term gains persist. OpenAI notes that analysis is ongoing and engagement varied.

IBM · September 2, 2026

AI readiness in K–12 schools

Morning Consult survey · United States
2,048 adults

IBM commissioned Morning Consult to survey 2,048 US adults in July 2026: 1,019 K–12 education professionals and 1,029 parents of K–12 students. IBM reports that only 20% of K–12 educators had received extensive AI training.

The survey also reports weekly classroom AI use by 76% of middle-school educators and 73% of high-school educators.

DGBP relevance: The findings raise questions about educator preparation, responsible use and the gap between adoption and readiness. They describe a US survey, not Indian schools.

Apple · iPad in Education

Interactive technology and learning examples

Company education evidence collection
16.4%

Apple's education evidence PDF describes a Stanford-associated study in which students who spent 120 minutes of class time playing the Wuzzit Trouble mathematics game improved their math problem-solving skills by 16.4% in the reported comparison.

The same collection describes other educational app studies, including an early-literacy study involving 82 children across seven Head Start classrooms.

DGBP relevance: Interactive learning may be worth testing, but these are selected examples in an Apple-published collection. They should not be interpreted as proof that every tablet or app improves learning.

Independent academic evidence

Research beyond company claims

Peer-reviewed studies can test specific learning questions under defined conditions. They still have boundaries: population, subject, intervention, measurement and study design matter.

PLOS ONE · May 24, 2024

ChatGPT-generated help and mathematics learning

Peer-reviewed randomized study · N = 274
274 participants

Pardos and Bhandari compared ChatGPT-generated help, human tutor-authored help and no-help control conditions across four mathematics areas: elementary algebra, intermediate algebra, college algebra and statistics.

The study found statistically significant learning gains for the ChatGPT-help condition compared with no help, with no statistically significant difference between ChatGPT and human tutor help. The authors also reported that 32% of generated hints failed quality checks before mitigation.

DGBP relevance: Examine whether explanations and hints support learning, while checking accuracy and avoiding blind reliance on generated answers.

PNAS · Published 2025

Generative AI without learning safeguards

High-school mathematics field experiment · Nearly 1,000 students
~1,000 students

Researchers studied two AI tutor designs in high-school mathematics: a GPT-4 interface resembling a standard chatbot and a tutor designed with prompts to safeguard learning.

AI access improved practice performance. On a later unassisted assessment, students in the standard GPT Base group performed 17% worse than the control group; the safeguarded GPT Tutor group did not show the same statistically significant decline.

DGBP relevance: Assisted performance is not the same as independent learning. Assessments should test whether learners can solve problems after assistance is removed.

DGBP research implications

Turning external evidence into our own questions

01 · Understanding vs memorisation

Can learners explain a concept and use it in a new situation, rather than only recognise a correct option?

02 · Assisted vs independent

What changes when a learner completes a task with help compared with completing it independently?

03 · Retention over time

Can learners recall and apply concepts after a meaningful delay?

04 · Topic-level performance

Which topic patterns are hidden by an overall score?

05 · Application and reasoning

Can learners transfer knowledge to unfamiliar questions and practical problems?

06 · Study technique evaluation

Which learning approaches are associated with better comprehension, recall and application under comparable conditions?

Evidence limitations and responsible interpretation

Company surveys are often commissioned or published by the company and may measure reported behaviour, not causal learning effects. Programme reach does not equal proven academic improvement. Academic experiments have specific samples, subjects and conditions; their findings may not generalise to every learner, school or country. DGBP presents these sources as external references, not as research conducted by DGBP. DGBP's personal pilot figures are separate and must not be compared directly with these studies without equivalent populations, tests and conditions.

Source review date: 9 November 2026. Links point to the source publisher or journal record. Figures and summaries are limited to the information reported by those sources.
